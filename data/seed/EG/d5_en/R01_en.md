# Hypothetical Judgment for Demonstration Purposes - R01
Court: Cairo Economic Court (hypothetical judgment)

English translation of R01.md.

---

## Facts

The facts of the present dispute are that the plaintiff, Mr. "Ahmed Mahmoud Ali", brought this action against the defendant, "Nile Bank for Investment and Finance" (in its official capacity), seeking an order requiring the bank to refund amounts collected as "late-payment penalties" on a personal financing he had obtained from the bank.

On review of the case file, it was established that the plaintiff entered into a personal financing contract with the defendant bank dated 15 January 2022, to obtain a specified financing amount for consumer purposes. The contract between the parties included clauses on the repayment mechanism and the schedule of monthly instalments, as well as a clause imposing late-payment penalties if the customer failed to meet the agreed payment dates.

The documents submitted by the plaintiff indicate that, while paying his instalments, he was surprised by deductions of additional amounts labelled as late-payment penalties that exceeded the agreed rates or those customary in such transactions, resulting in an unjustified build-up of debt on his account. The plaintiff based his claim on the argument that these penalties constitute an onerous financial burden and exceed the bounds of fairness and contractual logic, and requested that the penalties be recalculated at a reasonable rate and that the sums unduly collected be refunded.

The case was referred for pleadings, each party submitted its memoranda and documents, and the court accordingly reserved the case for judgment.

## Plaintiff's Claims

On the basis of the foregoing, the plaintiff summarised his claims as follows:
1. Appointment of an accounting expert to determine the total amounts collected as late-payment penalties over the financing period.
2. An order requiring the defendant bank to refund EGP 47,300 (forty-seven thousand three hundred Egyptian pounds), representing the difference between the penalties actually collected and the penalties the plaintiff considers fair.
3. An order requiring the bank to bear the costs and legal fees.

## Defence of the Defendant Bank

The defendant bank responded to the plaintiff's allegations, relying on the following defences:
1. The binding force of the contract between the parties, asserting that the plaintiff signed all clauses of the financing contract of his own free will, including the late-payment penalty clause, and that the bank did not act outside the terms of the contract.
2. The bank argued that the penalties imposed constitute agreed compensation for the damage caused by the plaintiff's failure to pay his instalments on time, which disrupted the bank's expected cash flows.
3. The bank asserted that all calculations were carried out using the bank's approved automated systems and in accordance with the terms of the financing contract, denying any error in the calculation of the amounts.
4. In concluding its defence, the bank requested that the claim be dismissed in its entirety for lack of a sound legal basis.

## Reasons for the Judgment

Having heard the pleadings, reviewed the documents and deliberated in accordance with the law, the court finds as follows:

**First: As to the subject matter of the dispute:**
It is established in law that the contract is the law of the parties. However, this principle is not absolute, as a contract is subject to judicial review where it contains abusive terms or late-payment penalties that go beyond fair compensation for damage and become a means of unjust enrichment at the debtor's expense.

**Second: Examination of the late-payment penalty clause:**
On examining the financing contract between the parties, the court found that the bank applied a very high late-payment penalty rate when the plaintiff was late in paying. Although the plaintiff's signature on the contract is valid, the court considers that this rate exceeds compensation for the actual damage suffered by the bank as a result of the delay, and falls within the scope of abusive terms that require judicial intervention to amend them so as to restore contractual balance.

**Third: Assessment of a fair penalty:**
Based on the settled principles of economic jurisprudence, a penalty must be proportionate to the damage. Since the plaintiff has proven that amounts were collected in excess of a reasonable limit, the court has decided to reduce the late-payment penalty rate to 2% per month, which the court considers fair and proportionate to the nature of personal financing and the bank's expected damage.

**Fourth: The amount to be refunded:**
On reviewing the accounts and auditing the amounts collected from the plaintiff, a financial difference was found resulting from the application of the previous (higher) rate compared with the rate set by the court (2% per month). After carrying out estimated calculations, the court is satisfied that the total amount the bank must refund to the plaintiff as the difference in late-payment penalties is EGP 47,300.

Since the plaintiff's claims were well founded in principle but included amounts exceeding what was actually due to him according to the court's assessment, the court partially dismisses the claim as to any amount exceeding what is due, and upholds it as regards the refund of the calculated difference.

## Operative Part

**The court rules:**

1. **To uphold the claim in part, and to dismiss it as to the remainder.**
2. **To order the defendant bank (Nile Bank for Investment and Finance) to pay the plaintiff, Mr. "Ahmed Mahmoud Ali", the sum of EGP 47,300 (forty-seven thousand three hundred Egyptian pounds), representing the difference in late-payment penalties collected in excess of the 2% monthly rate.**
3. **To order the defendant to bear the costs and legal fees.**
